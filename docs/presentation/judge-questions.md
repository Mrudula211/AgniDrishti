# Anticipated Judge Questions

Status: Draft · Last updated: 2026-09-29

Answer outlines for the idea round and finale. Numbers come only from recorded runs
(all **synthetic**; provenance in [idea-submission-content.md](idea-submission-content.md)).
Test run: `artifacts/metrics/E1-E6_test/20260926-144448_c8ca99b`.

| # | Question | Answer outline | Backing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Why not an LSTM / Transformer? | 2–4 readings per part: nothing for recurrence or attention to exploit, many parameters vs few observations, weak explainability. Level and drift features capture it directly. We would reconsider with dense data and a measured baseline gap. | M-01; ablation E3 |
| Q2 | Is this novel? | The parts are established (DPAT, ESCC drift limits, burn-in ML, conformal, reject option); DPAT is our Module A baseline. Our contribution: lot-relative drift at 24 h, a calibrated 168 h range, a mandatory REVIEW state with the fired rule as the explanation, and an ablation that shows what each part adds. | novelty-boundaries.md |
| Q3 | What data did you use? | Synthetic PS-shaped lots (no official dataset exists), always labelled. NASA PCoE data is downloaded for a methodology check that has not been run yet. Never presented as ISRO data. | data-sources.md |
| Q4 | Didn't you detect the defects you injected? | Partly, and we say so: test lots come from the development behaviour families. Two further families were written outside the repo and sealed by SHA-256 on 26 Sep 2026, before any detector code; they are not yet opened and are the final unseen test. The test lots were evaluated once. | synthetic-data-design §7 |
| Q5 | What does your range mean? | A 90 % prediction interval targeting marginal coverage of the true 168 h value; measured 0.887 on test lots. Per-lot coverage varied 0.668–0.980 on validation, so Phase 2 gates on per-lot coverage. Not a probability of failure. | ablation-plan §3, F4 |
| Q6 | Can you guarantee zero false negatives? | No. Recall 0.80 (95 % CI 0.63–0.91, 30 positives): 6 of 30 escaped, all late-onset shapes that look healthy at 24 h. The 96 h re-screen is the planned answer. | F5 |
| Q7 | How many parts go to REVIEW? | 10.4 % of rows at the E6 operating point; REVIEW + REJECT is 11.0 % and holds 24 of 30 drifting rows (≈ 7× the base rate). | test run |
| Q8 | Why not stop at E3? It has higher precision. | E3: precision 0.213 at 3.5 % review; E6: 0.092 at 10.4 %. The PS treats a missed defect as costlier than a review, so E6 is the recall-first point: +4 catches for about 165 more reviews. The review threshold is re-chosen when the pipeline is fit on a site's history, to meet the target recall set in config (`operating_point.target_recall`), so a site with a different cost balance gets a different point. | ADR-005; experiments.select_operating_point |
| Q9 | Why does DPAT (E2) lower precision vs E1? | It adds flags without adding catches on this data: its lot-level outliers are mostly stable parts, which our drift-based label does not count (scenario-truth recall does rise 0.055 → 0.127). That is why it is labelled a baseline, not the detector. | F2 |
| Q10 | 6 of 14 REJECTs were wrong — isn't that dangerous? | All 6 are healthy parts with one glitchy 24 h reading; with two points a spike looks like a jump. REJECT is a recommendation the engineer confirms or overrides (logged). Proposed v2: single-jump cases → REVIEW, to be tested on fresh pre-registered data. | F6 |
| Q11 | Your label uses drift — the same thing the detector measures. | True. There is no ground truth for PS 26170; the proxy follows ESCC-style drift limits (drift > 15 % of the 0 h value by 168 h, or 168 h outside the limit). Scenario-truth recall is reported alongside it. | ADR-004 |
| Q12 | Why keep the forecast if it added no detection? | Module B MAE is an official PS metric (it beats persistence: Iddq 0.297 vs 0.338 µA on validation), and R2/R3 use its drift rate. We report that it added no recall here rather than hide it. | F3 |
| Q13 | How is the safety slope calculated? | The PS does not define it: s = Δallow / 168 h with one Δallow per site config (15 % of V0 in the prototype, applied to every parameter); drift rate r = (Ŷ168 − V0)/168 h; R2 needs both the point and the optimistic end of the range above s. The E0 audit reports the label share across a pre-registered Δ grid. Per-parameter Δ values are not implemented yet. | ADR-002; decision.exceeds_safety_slope |
| Q21 | Your ESCC reference gives IDD ±75 nA; your Δallow is 15 % of V0 (≈ 1.3 µA here). Why so loose? | Different device (a CMOS 4049UB detail spec vs our synthetic part), and the 15 % is a prototype setting, not an ISRO value. The rule accepts an absolute Δ, but the pipeline holds one allowance per site, so per-parameter ESCC-style Δ limits are the next step and must come from the part's detail spec. | ADR-002; S-03 |
| Q22 | ISRO already screens drift with Δ-from-0 h limits at 168 h. Is your 4/30 baseline fair? | Our E1 applies the datasheet limit at 24 h — the same decision time as AgniDrishti — which the slides now say. A fixed ESCC-style Δ limit applied at 24 h is the right extra baseline; it has not been run, and our v1 test split is spent, so it needs a new pre-registered config and fresh data. The claim we make is earlier (24 h) and lot-relative, not "beats current 168 h practice". | ablation-plan §5 |
| Q23 | The PS asks for a regression model. Is yours one? | We compared four forecasters on validation MAE: persistence (V168 = V24) 0.338, linear extrapolation 1.174, least-squares regression on (V0, V24) 0.408, and a learned population increment (V24 + median 24→168 h rise from training lots) 0.297 µA (Iddq). The regression was built and lost, so the simpler learned model is used — every layer has to earn its place. | fit_summary.md; predict.py |
| Q14 | Where do 2.5 σ and 6 σ come from? | 6 σ is AEC-Q001 DPAT practice (median ± 6·IQR/1.35); 2.5 σ was chosen on validation lots from a pre-registered grid to approach the recall target. | pipeline_v1.yaml; ADR-005 |
| Q15 | How fast / scalable is it? | Measured on one PC: 2,368 rows 0.35 s, 23,680 rows 2.6 s, 118,400 rows (500 lots) 14.2 s — linear. Lots are independent. | service runs 2026-09-29 |
| Q16 | What if a lot is small, bimodal or mostly defective? | Lots under 20 parts, degenerate spread (MAD/IQR = 0) or missing values → quality gate → REVIEW, never PASS. Robust statistics tolerate a minority of defects, not a majority; the absolute limit and safety slope remain as backstops. | FR-02; R0 |
| Q17 | How do you prevent leakage? | Lot-grouped splits, frozen test lots evaluated once, automated tests that forbid 96 h / 168 h values in 24 h features. | evaluation-protocol.md |
| Q18 | Where is the AI? | Fitted from data: forecast chosen by held-out MAE, range calibrated by split conformal, review threshold chosen to a recall target — deliberately simple for 2–4 readings per part, with an exact, rule-based explanation. | ml-pipeline.md |
| Q20 | I regenerated your data and the hash differs. | The pre-registered hash (7a170d89…) reproduces on Windows and on Ubuntu 22.04 with numpy 1.26 / 2.4 and pandas 2.2 / 2.3 (checked 2026-09-29). The CSV keeps full float precision, so a platform whose vectorised maths differs in the last bit gives a different hash, and the run refuses to start by design. Use the exact dataset file attached to the release and check its hash. | README "Reproduce the deck's numbers" |
| Q19 | How would ISRO deploy it? | Offline service on a site PC: fit on the site's historical lots (`scripts/fit_pipeline.py`), map tester columns in the site config, screen each lot at 24 h; audit record + replay for every run. Not yet run on ISRO data. | deployment.md; ADR-006/007 |
